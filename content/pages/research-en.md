# Neural Basis of Visually-Guided Decisions in Freely Moving Mice

Exploring our surroundings often requires continuous recognition of familiar places to guide our spatial decisions. While this process feels effortless, it actually requires complex integration of sensory cues (the spatial context) with mnemonic information (known directions) in order to execute precise motor actions, such as turning at an intersection or continuing forward. The intricate nature of this system is perhaps more evident when it fails. For instance, in neurodegenerative diseases where patients become disoriented even in familiar settings.

Historically, technological limitations have made it difficult to study cortical activity during free exploration. Modern advances, however, now allow us to track specific visual inputs that animals encounter during active navigation. Thus, by combining miniature cameras with electrophysiology and calcium imaging, our lab aims to investigate how mice process the specific visual features that drive their spatial decisions in a naturalistic manner, during unconstrained behavior.
